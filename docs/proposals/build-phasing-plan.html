The organising principle

Every phase ends with a verb you gain

The failure mode for a project this size is a sequence of infrastructure phases that produce nothing usable until month nine. So each phase below is named for what you can do at the end of it that you could not do at the start. If a phase cannot be named that way, it is the wrong phase boundary.

Three rules follow from that, and they shaped the ordering more than anything else:

  1. Retrofit-hostile things go first. Units, coordinate frames and the file format are cheap now and expensive later. Nothing else is allowed to precede them.
  2. A commitment is a dependency. COMMIT-01 says no optimiser result ships on estimator evidence alone. That single sentence forces the VLM tier to exist before the goal-driven experience — which is not the order anyone would pick by enthusiasm.
  3. Validate before you build on it. The estimator is validated against experimental data in Phase 1, before four phases of UI are built on top of it.

On the numbers. Week counts are indicative and assume one developer working steadily on a personal project. Treat the ratios as the signal, not the absolute weeks — that P3 is three times P1, and P8 is the largest single item, is the useful information here.

The phases

What each one gates, closes and delivers

P02 weeks

Conventions & spine

Gate to start: none. This is the front of the queue by definition.

Contents. The units, coordinate-frame and sign-convention specification — origin, axis directions, the sign of anhedral and twist, and units carried in the type system rather than in comments. The station-and-loft geometry model with its two layers. The file format, which is load-bearing because it is the only import path. A test strategy for numerical code: analytic reference cases, golden masters, tolerance comparison.

You can: define a wing and get span, area, aspect ratio and mean chord out of it, with tests that prove they are right.

Closes GAP-04, GAP-08, GAP-09. Honours COMMIT-03. — This is the one phase whose deliverable is thin, and it is worth it: a sign convention retrofitted after four phases of code is how sign errors become permanent.

P13 weeks

Estimator, validated

Gate to start: P0 complete.

Contents. The section catalog — 11 already vendored with hashes and measured geometry, plus the Selig/Lednicer format detection that a defect already taught us to need. Section polars retaining Cp_min and Ncrit. The closed-form chain: Helmbold, lifting-line induced drag, ITTC friction, Hoerner form factor, cavitation critical speed. Then validation against DTIC ADA032272 — real towing-tank lift and drag for NACA 16-309 and 64A309.

You can: hand it a wing definition and get L/D, Cl/Cd, required α and cavitation margin — headless, scriptable, and checked against experiment.

Closes GAP-01. — Headless on purpose. The core value is testable without a single pixel, and everything downstream consumes it. Validating here means four phases of UI are not built on an unverified number.

P24 weeks

Read a wing

Gate to start: GAP-03 — a provisional answer on who the user is. Not a full research programme; enough to know whether the first screen speaks engineer or rider.

Contents. The WPF shell. The chart set — Cp distribution, drag polar, Cl/Cd/L/D against α, the cavitation bucket, transition location — on ScottPlot. Discipline presets from the geometry catalog. Salt and fresh as an ITTC fluid record. Overlay comparison, because comparing two candidates is the actual task.

You can: open a wing and understand it. First genuinely useful product.

Analysis only — no editing yet. That boundary keeps the phase honest and shippable.

P36 weeks

Draw a wing

Gate to start: P2 complete. SPIKE-01 should run here — but it is not blocking, because its fallback (2D curve editing only) is the design the proposal already favours.

Contents. The five distribution curves — outline, chord, anhedral, thickness, twist — each a 2D editing problem with control points, handles, keyboard nudge at a defined step, and curvature combs always on. Degree-5 splines. The live properties strip running the estimator on every drag. HelixToolkit 3D viewport for display and selection.

You can: design a wing by hand and see the hydrodynamic consequence of a control-point move at the moment you make it.

This is arguably the product. At the end of P3 you are 38% of the way through the effort and you have a tool nothing else on the market offers — live L/D and cavitation margin while shaping.

P47 weeks

Trust the numbers

Gate to start: P1 complete (needs the estimator to disagree with).

Contents. The vortex-lattice tier over the same stations — real spanwise loading, computed span efficiency instead of an assumed constant, and stabiliser downwash so a rear wing is evaluated in the front wing's wake rather than free stream. The quasi-3D section view driven by α_eff, not the geometric angle. Multi-fidelity reconciliation: what the UI shows when two tiers disagree. And a beam structural model taking loads from the VLM tier.

You can: trust a number enough to cut material — and ask whether the section is thick enough to survive, which until now nothing could answer.

Closes GAP-02, GAP-05 partly, GAP-06, GAP-11. Unblocks COMMIT-01. — Structures lands here because it needs aero loads, and because it turns thickness from a user-set constraint with a displayed cost into a solved quantity.

P54 weeks

State a goal

Gate to start: P4 complete — hard gate. COMMIT-01 forbids presenting an optimiser result on estimator evidence alone, so the verification tier must exist first.

Contents. The constraint / objective / context model as three distinct input types. NSGA-II or MOPSO over the estimator with feasibility-first ranking. The four-pane screen, Pareto set, preference brushing, and the pin-and-compare ghost line. Every optimum re-checked at the VLM tier before it is shown, with a confidence beside the score.

You can: describe what you want and get candidates that have been checked before you see them.

The search itself is free — 20,000 evaluations in 0.2 seconds. The work here is interaction design, not optimisation.

P63 weeks

Build the wing

Gate to start: SPIKE-02 — a STEP file a real CAM system opens with a smooth surface. Needs only P0's geometry model, so this can run in parallel from week 15.

Contents. STEP AP203 export of the lofted B-spline surface, own writer or STEPcode. STL and 3MF for printing and meshing. And manufacturing constraints fed back into the design space — minimum trailing-edge thickness, moldability, layup floor. Our measured sections currently have trailing edges of 0–0.08 mm, which is unbuildable as drawn.

You can: hand the file to a machinist.

Closes GAP-07. Honours COMMIT-02 — permissive licences only, no kernel required.

P73 weeks

Explain it

Gate to start: SPIKE-04 — the numeral check must exist before any explanation ships. Individual capabilities can be pulled forward: brief→intent alongside P5, vocabulary bridging as early as P2.

Contents. The numeral check first. Then explain-the-design, then brief→intent with per-field provenance flags, then knowledge-base Q&A and the vocabulary bridge. BYOK key handling. Every capability with its eval.

You can: ask why, and get an answer that cites its sources and cannot invent a number.

Honours COMMIT-04. — Additive by construction: no key, no network, and everything else still works.

P88 weeks

Prove it · the CFD tier

Gate to start: SPIKE-03 — can meshing be generated unattended across the geometry range? This is the load-bearing assumption of the whole orchestration story.

Contents. The ICfdBackend boundary. WSL2 substrate, case generation, process orchestration with streaming residuals. OpenFOAM and SU2. The CUDA LBM tier for free-surface proximity, separation and ventilation onset — toolchain already proven on this machine. 3D flow visualisation from the same VRAM buffers.

You can: see the free surface, which nothing else in this category can do.

The one open physics risk sits here: LBM accuracy above Re 10⁶ is still unproven, and our envelope is 5.5×10⁵–1.6×10⁶. The first deliverable of this phase is a comparison, not a solver.

04

The problem with this ordering

The differentiator is last

Free surface is the thing that justifies building anything at all — and in this plan it arrives at week 32, if it arrives.

The gap review was explicit: after the surfboard scope cut, the case for this project rests on two gaps, and free surface is the load-bearing one. No small tool models it; hydrofoils run shallow where it dominates. If P8 never happens, what you have built is a very good XFLR5-for-hydrofoils — genuinely useful, and not the thing the project was justified on.

Three ways to respond, and I would take the third:

  1. Accept it. A better XFLR5 with live estimation, a measured section catalog, cavitation margin and STEP export is a real tool. If it stops there, that is not a failure. But decide that deliberately rather than discovering it at week 32.
  2. Move P8 earlier. Honest about the priority, but it means building the most expensive tier before the cheap ones that answer most questions — and before the geometry model has settled under real use.
  3. Insert a de-risking slice at ~week 15. Not the tier — one case. Take a single wing, mesh it by hand, run one free-surface simulation at a shallow submergence, and check whether the lift-loss trend is credible. One to two weeks to learn whether the project's central claim survives contact with a solver. If it does, P8 is derisked. If it does not, you have learned it seventeen weeks early and the whole plan changes.

Option 3 costs about 4% of the total and tests the assumption the other 96% rests on. That is the same reasoning that made Phase 0 measurement worth doing, and it paid off there.

Three natural stopping points

P3 — draw a wing38% A hand-design tool with a measured section catalog, live L/D and cavitation margin while shaping, and validated estimation behind it. Nothing on the market does this. No verification tier, so the numbers carry estimator confidence only. No export, no optimiser, no free surface

P6 is the honest v1 boundary. It closes the loop from a stated intent to a file a machinist can cut, and everything after it is enhancement rather than completion.

Before /specify

What still needs your input

Four things this plan assumes and cannot decide for you. Each changes the phasing if answered differently:

  • Who is the first user — you, a shaper, or a rider? (GAP-03.) It gates P2 and reorders P7: if riders matter, vocabulary bridging moves from Phase 7 to Phase 2 and becomes a headline feature rather than a convenience.
  • Is fabrication actually in scope, or is this an analysis tool? If nobody machines a wing, P6 disappears, SPIKE-02 is unnecessary, and the STEP question closes entirely.
  • How much does the free surface really matter to you? If it is the whole point, take option 3 in section 04 and de-risk it at week 15. If it is a nice-to-have, P8 becomes optional and the plan shortens by 20%.
  • What is the actual time budget? Forty weeks of steady work is a big personal project. If it is evenings and weekends, the phase boundaries still hold but the stopping points in section 05 become the real decisions.

One process recommendation. Run /adddomainexperts before /specify. It has been recommended after every knowledge pass and never run, so no marine-hydrodynamics, structures or GPU lens has reviewed any of this — and the gap review found that a structures lens would have caught GAP-02 far earlier. It is cheap, and it improves everything downstream of it.
