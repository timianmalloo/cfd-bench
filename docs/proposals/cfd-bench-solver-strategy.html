01

What changed

The scope cut, and what it costs

Surfboards and planing are out. Retired with them: the Savitsky empirical method, RANS+VOF with 6-DOF trim and sinkage, and the whole free-surface-craft cost rung. The knowledge base keeps those passages marked RETIRED rather than deleting them, because the architecture was reasoned from them.

This costs something and it should be said plainly. Planing was one of three gaps that justified building anything at all. The case against building was re-tested on the narrower scope — it still holds, but on two gaps rather than three, and it now leans heavily on the free-surface argument alone.

What v2 adds is more than compensation: a closed-form estimation chain that was executed, not just described, a section catalog with a citable practitioner ranking, a geometry catalog per discipline, and a four-concept parametric grammar. The shape of the recommendation changed as a result — see section 07.

02

The reframe

Most of this is not a simulation problem

The central v2 finding is that a chain of published closed forms answers most of the design question outright. Each step is sourced; the chain was run against real water-sports geometry and the numbers come out physically right.

One source was found internally inconsistent, and re-derivation caught it. The IHS gives V_crit = 14/√σᵢ and separately advises taking vapour pressure as 17,000 Pa for salt water. Deriving the constant from first principles gives 13.94 using ITTC's actual value of 1,670.9 Pa, and 12.82 using 17,000. The formula is right; the quoted vapour pressure is off by a factor of ten and is not a physical property.

The general lesson is worth keeping: when a source gives both a formula and its inputs, re-derive — the redundancy is a free consistency check.

03

The chain, executed

Efficiency across the four disciplines

Every curve peaks between 10 and 11.5 knots, which is not a coincidence: a fixed rider weight on a fixed area drives the lift coefficient down as speed rises, and peak efficiency sits where induced and friction drag balance. Past that point every foil is over-winged for its own speed.

Higher aspect ratio lifts the whole curve — the downwind foil at AR 10.5 reaches 22.2 against the surf foil's 16.4. That is the entire reason the downwind discipline drove the industry toward AR 9–12.

What the run establishesEvidence
The geometry falls out correctly without tuning spans 95–107 cm, chords 8–19 cm — matching real production foils
The induced/friction crossover is the story of the disciplines CDᵢ is 77% of drag for surf at 7 kn, 3% for race at 28 kn
Take-off asks more of the section than the design point surf needs CL 0.78 at 7 kn vs the 0–0.6 typical band
Cavitation does not bind below ~25 kn σ stays above 3 until 22 kn; reaches 1.0 only at 28 kn
Span is nearly fixed across the domain 95–107 cm while area varies 2.5× and AR 3×

That last point has a direct interface consequence: area and aspect ratio are the real design variables and span is a consequence, so the tool should let the user drive area and AR and show span as an output — not the reverse.

04

Sections

The catalog, and how to choose from it

The International Hydrofoil Society compilation states the design problem in a directly usable form. Cavitation begins where pressure is lowest, so you minimise the maximum velocity — no sharp pressure peaks — while keeping the average velocity over the upper surface high enough to lift. That drives sections toward:

Long, flat pressure distributions — shaped like a building with a flat roof.

This "rooftop" criterion is the most useful selection rule in the domain, and it is computable: it is a property of the XFOIL pressure distribution, not a matter of taste.

Why separation matters more than it looks. The IHS lists four necessary conditions for ventilation — sub-atmospheric local pressure, an air path, separated flow, and a stable cavity — and identifies separation as the key one. With fully attached flow, introduced air simply streams off as bubbles without ventilating. So the 6-series recommendation is not an efficiency preference: avoiding trailing-edge separation is how you avoid the failure mode that collapses lift.

What a section catalog must store, and what most databases omit

Declaring the grain first: one row is one (section, source revision) pair for geometry, and a separate table holds one row per (section, Re, Ncrit, alpha) for polars. Geometry is a measurement; polars are re-derivable. Keeping them apart is what lets you regenerate polars without touching provenance.

  • Cp_min must be retained. It is the sole input to the cavitation check, and it is the value airfoil databases most commonly discard.
  • Ncrit must be recorded. A polar without its transition parameter is not reproducible.
  • Coordinates need provenance and a revision. Sets in circulation differ in point count and trailing-edge treatment, and those differences change XFOIL results. Treat the coordinate file as data with a source, not as a constant.
  • Thickness and camber are derived from coordinates, never stored beside them. Two definitions of one quantity is the defect signature here.
05

Geometry

A four-concept parametric grammar

A complete foil assembly needs exactly four ideas. Everything in the domain falls out of them, with no special cases.

ConceptDefinitionWhat it absorbs
AssemblyOrdered set of surfaces, plus fluid and operating pointThe whole craft
SurfaceOrdered stations, a span axis, a loft rule, optional mirrorFront wing, stabiliser, and strut — a strut is just a surface with a vertical span axis
StationPosition (x,y,z), chord, incidence, sectionTaper, washout, anhedral, sweep — all become station-to-station variation
Loft ruleLinear or spline interpolation between stationsSurface generation

Decalage falls out rather than being modelled. It is the incidence difference between front-wing and stabiliser stations. Fuselage length is the x separation between surfaces — a consequence of station positions, not a stored field. This mirrors how OpenVSP works, where tails, fins and strakes are all created from the same wing object; that is corroboration, not coincidence.

# explicit layer — the geometry of record
assembly "Downwind 1100" {
  fluid    seawater @ 15C
  operate  speed 12kn  mass 95kg  depth 0.45m

  surface front {
    axis y   mirror true   loft spline
    station y=0.000  chord=0.130  inc=+1.5  section=E817
    station y=0.300  chord=0.108  inc=+1.0  section=E817
    station y=0.535  chord=0.045  inc=+0.2  section=E818  z=-0.075
  }
  surface stab {
    axis y   mirror true   loft linear
    station y=0.000  chord=0.075  inc=-1.0  section=naca:0010  x=0.620
    station y=0.180  chord=0.040  inc=-1.0  section=naca:0010  x=0.620
  }
  surface strut {
    axis z   loft linear          # the only difference from a wing
    station z=0.000  chord=0.140  inc=0  section=naca:0012
    station z=0.750  chord=0.120  inc=0  section=naca:0012
  }
}

Two layers, one direction of flow

The syntax above is explicit — full control, verbose. Nobody wants to move eleven numbers to try a higher aspect ratio, so a generative layer sits above it and emits stations:

surface front from planform {
  area 1100cm2   aspect 10.5   taper 0.35
  sweep 4deg     anhedral 8deg  washout 1.3deg   section E817
}
  • The generative layer is the design vector — roughly 6–8 numbers per surface plus CST coefficients. Small enough for an optimiser to search, expressive enough to matter.
  • The explicit layer is the geometry of record. Estimator, mesher, solver and export read only stations, and never need to know whether a human or a generator wrote them.
  • Generation is one-way. A generated surface can be burst to explicit stations for hand editing; stations are never back-fitted to planform parameters. Two-way sync between a parametric description and its output is a known source of silent divergence, and one direction removes the entire class.

Sections: CST, and why the parameterisation debate dissolves

The Class-Shape Transformation writes a section as a class function ψ^N1(1−ψ)^N2 — with N1=0.5, N2=1 giving the airfoil class, round leading edge and sharp trailing edge — times a Bernstein-polynomial shape function. Invalid airfoils are unrepresentable by construction, which keeps validation out of the solver.

And the apparent choice between methods is not real: NACA 4-series, CST and PARSEC are all exactly equivalent to Bézier curves. Pick CST because its class/shape split is the most direct, and treat the others as conversions rather than competing systems needing separate code paths.

Derive, never store

Area, aspect ratio, span, mean chord and wetted area are computed from stations. None is stored. This is not fastidiousness — manufacturers publish aspect ratios computed by inconsistent conventions, so the same wing carries different published numbers depending on whether area is projected, developed or planform. Computing from stations under one stated convention is exactly what makes this tool's numbers comparable when the market's are not.

06

Phase 0 — complete

What measurement changed

Phase 0 targeted four evidence gaps. Three closed by measurement; the GPU questions need a privileged install and stay open. The largest gap in the knowledge base closed by showing the question was malformed.

Thickness is a structural variable with a hydrodynamic price, not a parameter with an optimum. Hydrodynamics always prefers thinner; what stops you is bending stiffness. The retail guidance was not wrong about the band — it was wrong about the reason, and calling it an optimum sends a designer hunting a maximum that does not exist.

The price is now quantified, which is what makes it usable: 9% → 12% t/c costs ~14% of section L/D and 3.8 knots of cavitation-free speed. So the tool should ask — live — "is your structure worth 14% efficiency and 4 knots?" rather than offering a number to optimise.

Corroboration from independent data: the five cambered Eppler hydrofoil sections measure 7.90–10.98% t/c, exactly what "as thin as the structure allows" predicts.

  1. The Eppler sections deliver on cavitation, not on raw efficiency. E818 reaches 42.1 kn against NACA 4412's 31.4 kn at identical loading — a 34% higher critical speed. This is the first quantitative confirmation of the "minimum cavitation" claim found anywhere in this research; every prior source simply asserted it.
  2. The practitioner's 6-series recommendation holds up under measurement. NACA 64A410 records the best section L/D in the set. The mechanism is visible in the numbers: 4412's σᵢ = 0.744 against 64A410's 0.496 is the suction peak the rooftop criterion exists to avoid.
  3. The catalog cannot have a single "best". E874 leads at Re 6×10⁵; E904 leads at Re 10⁶. Ranking must be per operating point — a ranked section list with no Reynolds number attached is misinformation.

Three more results worth carrying forward

  • Typhoon is Tornado VLM, MATLAB, GPL v2+. Its source header says so verbatim. That reclassifies it: copyleft and a MATLAB runtime rule out reuse, so it is a reference and a validation benchmark, not a component — but it also independently corroborates Option 2, since the only open-source tool solving whole-craft hydrofoil equilibrium chose exactly the recommended method.
  • Section analysis needs no XFOIL binary. NeuralFoil (pure Python) validated against analytic truth — CL exactly 0 at zero incidence for a symmetric section, dCL/dα = 0.1066/deg against a theoretical 0.1097. Cp_min is not an output field but is recoverable from edge velocities as 1 − max(uᵉ/V∞)² — which is what makes every cavitation number above computable. Phase 1 loses a native dependency.
  • A defect was caught that would have corrupted the whole catalog. The first coordinate parser reported NACA 0012 as 6.00% thick with 3.00% camber — impossible for a symmetric section, which is 12% thick with none. Six of the eight Eppler files are Lednicer format, not Selig. It surfaced only because analytic NACA sections were used as a regression test before the Eppler numbers were trusted. The failure mode is the dangerous kind: plausible wrong numbers, not an error.

Two caveats that travel with every number above. NeuralFoil is a surrogate trained on XFOIL — trends and rankings are reliable, absolute drag carries model error. And Cp_min recovered from 32 discrete stations under-reads sharp suction peaks, biasing cavitation-free speed optimistically. For a safety-relevant check that is the wrong direction to err, so the tool must carry a margin and label the figure as a best case until a true pressure distribution replaces it.

07

Architecture

Five layers, and where cost lives

Geometry

Two-layer parametric model. Generative planform parameters emit explicit stations; stations are the record. CST or catalog sections. Append-only versioning so every result names the geometry that produced it.

—
Section data

Catalog with provenance, plus XFOIL for anything not precomputed. Polars keyed by (section, Re, Ncrit, alpha), retaining Cp_min. Cached, because the same section recurs constantly.

~100 ms
cold
Estimator

The product's inner loop. The closed-form chain: Helmbold, lifting line, ITTC friction, form factor, cavitation margin. Runs live against a dragged slider. Enforces its own validity envelope rather than documenting it.

~10 µs
Panel / VLM

Spanwise loading, non-elliptic planform effects, stabiliser downwash and the true front-wing/stab interaction. What the estimator's single span-efficiency number approximates.

~10–100 ms
GPU LBM

Free-surface proximity, submergence lift loss, separation, ventilation onset, 3D stall. Everything the low-order methods structurally cannot see. Validated against published experimental polars before it is trusted.

~1–5 min

The layers are strictly ordered by cost and each answers a question the one above cannot. Two design rules follow:

  1. Every layer reads explicit stations. No layer knows about the generative parameters. That keeps the geometry model from leaking into four different consumers.
  2. Disagreement between layers is a finding, not an error to hide. When the estimator and the LBM tier diverge, that divergence is information about where the closed forms stop being valid — and it is exactly what tells a user when to stop trusting the fast answer.

The .NET / CUDA boundary

Layers 1–4 are ordinary C#. Only layer 5 needs the GPU, and it sits behind a narrow interface — upload geometry and conditions, step, read back forces and a field slice. That narrowness is what makes the binding decision reversible: a native C++/CUDA library behind P/Invoke is the low-risk default, and if ILGPU turns out to support sm_120 it can replace the implementation without touching layers 1–4.

08

The decision

Revised options

The v1 options were framed around which solver to build. That framing was wrong for this scope. With the estimation chain established, the real question is how far up the cost ladder to go.

OPTION 1

Estimator and catalog only

Viable product

Layers 1–3. Parametric geometry, section catalog, closed-form estimation, WPF interface. No panel method, no GPU, no CUDA.

For

  • Ships in weeks. Pure C#, no native boundary, no toolkit.
  • Answers most real design questions, live.
  • Every component is a published closed form, so every component is exactly testable.
  • Already does things XFLR5 does not: cavitation margin, hydrofoil framing, discipline presets.

Against

  • Cannot see the free surface — the gap that justifies the project.
  • Span efficiency e is an assumed constant, not a computed result.
  • No stabiliser downwash; the two-surface interaction is approximated away.
OPTION 2

Estimator, catalog and panel/VLM

Recommended

Layers 1–4. Adds a vortex-lattice or panel solver over the same station geometry, computing real spanwise loading and front-wing/stabiliser interaction.

For

  • Removes the estimator's biggest approximation — span efficiency becomes computed rather than assumed.
  • Makes the two-surface problem real: downwash, decalage and pitch stability.
  • Still fast enough to feel interactive at ~10–100 ms.
  • Pure C#, no GPU, no native boundary. Same risk profile as Option 1.
  • The estimator becomes its fast preview and its regression oracle.

Against

  • Still no free surface. The core justification remains deferred.
  • Linear method — stall is still not represented.
  • More surface area to validate.
OPTION 3

Add the GPU LBM tier

The differentiator

All five layers. CUDA LBM for free-surface proximity, separation and ventilation onset.

For

  • The only option that reaches the actual gap. No small tool models the free surface, and hydrofoils run shallow where it dominates.
  • Uses hardware that comfortably fits the problem — a 43 M-cell foil box at an estimated ~220 steps/s.
  • Gives the low-order layers something to be validated against.

Against

  • LBM accuracy above Re 10⁶ is unproven for this use and must be demonstrated, not assumed.
  • Native interop boundary; requires CUDA Toolkit 12.8+.
  • Largest scope by a wide margin, and the last thing to deliver value.
OPTION 4

Wrap FluidX3D or OpenFOAM

Not recommended

Skip the low-order layers; drive an existing solver from a WPF front end.

For

  • Real validated physics with no solver to defend.

Against

  • Weakened considerably by v2. Most design questions here are closed-form — wrapping a batch solver answers the rare question and misses the common one.
  • FluidX3D is non-commercial only and has no adaptive mesh refinement.
  • No interactivity, which is the whole point of a design tool.

Recommendation: Option 2, sequenced so Option 1 ships first, with Option 3 gated on evidence.

The estimator is a complete product on its own and it is the layer users touch most. Panel/VLM is a natural, low-risk extension over the same geometry that removes the biggest approximation. The GPU tier should be built only after it has demonstrated accuracy against published experimental polars — it is the differentiator, but it is also the only part whose core assumption is unproven.

09

Sequencing

What to do, in what order

Phase 0done

Three of four gaps closed by measurement — the thickness question (no optimum exists; thickness is structural with a quantified price), the section catalog (11 sections vendored from UIUC with hashes and measured geometry), and Typhoon (Tornado VLM, MATLAB, GPL v2+). A working section-analysis toolchain was established with no XFOIL binary.

Still open, and each needs a CUDA Toolkit install: ILGPU on Blackwell, LBM accuracy above Re 10⁶, and this laptop’s real throughput. None blocks Phase 1, which touches no GPU.

Phase 1the product

Layers 1–3, end to end. Parametric geometry with the four-concept grammar, section catalog, closed-form estimator, WPF interface with live response. Discipline presets from the geometry catalog. Salt and fresh as an ITTC-sourced fluid record.

Every estimator step gets a unit test against a hand-worked value — Helmbold must approach 2π as AR grows, induced drag must match the elliptical result at e = 1, ITTC friction must match published tables.

Phase 2real 3D loading

Panel/VLM over the same stations. Spanwise loading, computed span efficiency, stabiliser downwash, decalage and pitch stability. The estimator stays as the fast preview and becomes the regression oracle — the two should agree within a known band, and where they do not, that is the finding.

Phase 3gated on proof

The GPU LBM tier — validation first. The first deliverable is not a solver but a comparison: a NACA section at a Reynolds number with published experimental polars, computed lift and drag set against them. Free surface and submergence follow only if that holds.

If drag proves unreliable at this Reynolds range while lift holds, the tool says so on screen. It does not report both as equally trustworthy.

10

The case against, re-tested

What survives the scope cut

The v1 counter-argument rested on three gaps in the existing free tools. The scope cut removes one, so the argument was re-run rather than carried forward.

GapStatus
Free surface — no small tool models submergence effects or ventilation Holds, and is now the load-bearing justification. Hydrofoils run shallow; lift falls with depth and ventilation can collapse it. XFLR5 and Typhoon cannot see this at all
Planing — no small tool handles a surfboard Retired with the scope cut
Integration — nothing joins sections, geometry, estimation and equilibrium interactively Holds, and v2 strengthened it. The estimation chain and catalog are real absent capabilities, not repackaging
New in v2: nothing couples a section catalog retaining Cp_min to a cavitation check and a live L/D estimate over parametric multi-surface geometry XFLR5 has polars but no cavitation model and no hydrofoil framing; Typhoon has equilibrium but an undocumented method; retail guides have discipline knowledge but no physics

Verdict: the case against survives the cut, but with less margin. The honest position is now smaller than v1's, and it should be stated that way rather than dressed up:

The defensible project is a design tool whose core is closed-form estimation over a parametric geometry model, with simulation reserved for free-surface and separation questions no low-order method can reach. It is not a CFD package. On the hydrofoil-only scope it is closer to a better XFLR5-for-hydrofoils than to a new solver — and that is a perfectly good thing to build, provided nobody pretends otherwise.
